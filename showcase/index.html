A robot guide dog gets a blind rider safely to their Waymo

Every scene is built from real Edgewater, Miami data. Streets, curbs, crossings and hazard locations are real data; the dog, rider, cars and anything tagged illustrative are simulated. The dog's depth and water sensing come from the simulator's ground truth; a trained YOLO model reads the walk signal.

  • OpenStreetMap streets, curbs, crossings
  • Miami-Dade 311 flood reports
  • FDOT work zones and crashes
  • NOAA / NWS tide and alerts

What getting around costs today

  • 1M Americans are blind; 7 million have vision impairment. CDC
  • $3,600 extra per intersection to add accessible (talking) pedestrian signals, $350 per button. U.S. Access Board, 2011
  • $2,500 average to fix one curb ramp. Baltimore alone has 37,317 non-compliant ramps. Baltimore DOT via Baltimore Brew, 2021
  • $150 is all a rider pays The Seeing Eye for a first guide dog; donors cover the rest. The Seeing Eye
  • $450 for the MechDog robot kit this prototype runs on. OzRobotics listing

Infrastructure is fixed one intersection at a time and guide dogs depend on donors. A robot guide travels with the rider and adds its own check at every curb, signal and puddle. It complements these, it doesn't replace them.

02 Crossing Biscayne Blvd with traffic

18.1 m, 5 lanes. The dog reads the pedestrian signal with its own camera, waits through DON'T WALK, and crosses only when WALK leaves enough time at a cane user's pace. It walks at a production escort's 0.9 m/s; today's $450 MechDog (0.42 m/s) could not finish this crossing in time (43.0 s needed vs 23.9 s of WALK + clearance).
